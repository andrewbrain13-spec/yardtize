## 1. The Anchor Property: 3103 Karnes Blvd, KCMO 64111

Real 2025 numbers from MoDOT's traffic database for that signalized corner:

|---|---|---|
| SW Trafficway | south of the 31st St signal | **33,316** |
| SW Trafficway | north of the 31st St signal | **25,244** |
| W 31st St | at SW Trafficway | **~11,070** |
| Karnes Blvd itself | — | no published count (minor street) |

**Roughly 36,000–44,000 vehicles pass that corner every day, and the signal means they stop there.** For context, AdQuick prices rural billboards seeing similar or less traffic at $250–$1,500 per 4-week period, and mid-metro boards at $1,500–$9,000. A yard sign is far smaller than a billboard, but at eye level with red-light dwell time, a few hundred dollars a month for that placement is defensible on straight CPM math. This corner is exactly what the "property-quality lane" means — and the fact that a campaign already puts a big sign there every cycle proves the demand at that exact spot.

Pricing nuance to keep in mind: research found ordinary interior-street yards clear at more like $10–50/month, and local campaigns are famously cheap (a $200/month ask equals ~30–50 printed signs to them). Both things are true: **premium corners are worth hundreds; average lawns are not.** The product should be built around finding and pricing the corners.

## 2. Your Four Questions, Answered

### Q: "Is there a size we can stay under?"

**Yes — and it's bigger than the standard yard sign.**

- **Kansas City, MO** (§ 88-445-06, verified from the city's official redline document): every house lot may display signs up to **8 sq ft each, 4 ft tall**, set back 5 ft from the right-of-way (corner lots: behind a diagonal 20 ft from the corner). Up to **16 sq ft aggregate** of noncommercial signage per lot, **no duration limit**, and signs **under 10 sq ft need no permit** (Building Code § 18-16). During the window from 6 weeks before to 2 weeks after a KC election, the aggregate cap is lifted for additional noncommercial signs.
- **Overland Park, KS** (Chapter 18.440, post-2020 content-neutral rewrite): up to **three stake signs totaling 9 sq ft** per residential lot (corner lots get one extra), reported duration up to **60 days** per placement, plus a separate for-sale sign allowance. *(Numbers from the city's ordinance coverage; exact adopted text lives at §18.440.130 and needs a browser-based pull — see §6.)*
- A standard 18"×24" yard sign is **3 sq ft** — comfortably legal everywhere in the metro. Even a 2'×4' (8 sq ft) sign fits KCMO's envelope.

### Q: "Does the sign being paid for change things?"

**No — payment is legally irrelevant. The message is everything.** Nothing in either city's code (or anything found anywhere) turns on whether money changed hands. What the codes regulate is the *content-to-premises relationship*:

- A **noncommercial message** (political, ideological, "Go Chiefs") is allowed on any permitted sign, paid or not. Payment does not strip First Amendment protection — the New York Times ad in *NYT v. Sullivan* was paid.
- A **commercial message for a business not operating on that lot** is prohibited in KCMO residential zones regardless of payment (§ 88-445-06-A.1.d: commercial messages "may not advertise or direct attention to a business or commercial activity other than one lawfully conducted on the premises") — and doubly so as off-premise advertising, banned citywide outside interstate corridors (§ 88-445-14).

So the paid political sign at 3103 Karnes is legally identical to a free one. The paid Pepsi sign in the same yard is a violation whether or not money moved. **This is why political-first isn't just a market choice — it's the legal architecture of the whole business.**

### Q: "Why don't contractors who drop a sign after a job get flagged?"

Three stacked reasons:

1. **It's expressly legal in KCMO.** § 88-445-06-A.2.b allows one "interim sign" per lot whose message relates to "the sale, lease, rental, or **construction** of the home" — up to 8 sq ft, no permit, and **no duration limit in the code**. The roofer's sign advertises work done *on that premises*, so it doesn't trip the off-premise prohibition.
2. **The discount-as-compensation question is simply not addressed.** No provision in either city keys on consideration. The roofer's discount doesn't reclassify the sign because classification never looks at payment.
3. **Enforcement is complaint-driven and de minimis violations are ignored.** KCMO enforces via 311 complaints through Neighborhood Preservation, with a warning letter and 10–30 days to cure before anything costs money. Overland Park runs notice → deadline → $140 reinspection fee → escalating from there. Nobody calls 311 about a tasteful sign in a neighbor's yard, and no case of a KC-metro homeowner actually fined over a yard sign could be found in any news or record searched.

*(The caution from Plan v1 stands: in roofing specifically, "advertising allowances" are often disguised insurance-deductible rebates, which are illegal in several states. Yardtize documenting these deals cleanly — real 1099'd payments for real placements, decoupled from any insurance claim — is a feature, not a bug.)*

### Q: "Is breaking the rules and letting the marketplace adjust acceptable, like Airbnb?"

**Short answer: you don't need to — and if you did, it wouldn't work here.**

The regulatory-entrepreneurship playbook (grow too big to ban, mobilize users, exploit gray areas) worked for Uber/Airbnb because violations were invisible (rides and stays happen behind closed doors), penalties hit a well-funded corporate defendant, and millions of users could be mobilized. Yard signs invert all three: violations are **visible from the street 24/7** (a code officer can cite a whole block from a car), citations land on **the homeowner** — your supply side, earning $50/month against up-to-$500/day exposure — and *City of Austin v. Reagan* (2022) hands any annoyed city council a content-neutral kill switch: reclassify paid third-party signage as off-premises advertising in one vote. Where cities put liability on individual suppliers, the playbook failed even for Uber (Austin 2016: driver cars impounded; NYC 2023: Airbnb host fines, listings collapsed).

**The twist: for political signs, current law is already on our side.** Standard-size political signs on consenting owners' property are compliant today in both cities, and in Kansas it's affirmatively protected — see §3. So the honest strategy is not civil disobedience; it's:

- **Stay inside the envelope** (≤8 sq ft KCMO / 3 signs & 9 sq ft OP, correct setbacks) so signs are legal-by-size.
- **48-hour takedown SLA** on any notice: since both cities start with notice-and-cure, instant takedown makes it nearly impossible for a host to ever reach a fine.
- **Yardtize fine guarantee:** we handle and pay any sign citation arising from a compliant placement. Expected cost ≈ $0; marketing value: high. (Precedent: Uber genuinely paid drivers' tickets during launches.) Indemnify good-faith placements only — never bankroll knowing violations.
- **Don't provoke the amendment.** The existential risk isn't today's enforcement; it's a city writing a new rule *because of us*. That means no ugly density (one tasteful sign per yard), no oversized signs, and being the platform that *removes* junk rather than adds it.

## 3. The Political Play (this is stronger than expected)

- **Kansas is the best political-sign jurisdiction imaginable.** K.S.A. 25-2711: from **45 days before until 2 days after any election**, no city or county may regulate *placement or number* of political signs on private property (only size/setback for sight lines). The Kansas Attorney General has enforced this *against cities* (threatened Wichita with legal action in 2023; Wichita folded). Overland Park cannot cap political signs during the window.
- **KCMO allows noncommercial signs year-round with no time limit** (16 sq ft aggregate, cap lifted near elections). Missouri's RSMo 442.404 blocks **HOAs** from prohibiting political signs, too.
- **Paying homeowners is a normal, reportable campaign expenditure.** FEC treats it as ordinary advertising spend (itemized over $200); Missouri and Kansas ethics rules likewise require reporting, not prohibition. Minnesota's campaign finance board has *affirmatively stated* candidates "may legitimately purchase such space" — for business-owned property, the *free* sign is the legal problem (in-kind contribution), not the paid one.
- **The bright lines:** (1) never condition payment on the homeowner's registration, party, or vote — payment is for space, period (vote-buying statutes); (2) signs need "Paid for by…" disclaimers; (3) hosts get W-9s and 1099s ($600+/yr), payments are taxable income; (4) run Yardtize as vendor-of-record so homeowners' names stay off public campaign reports — better privacy, cleaner books.
- **The calendar:** the Aug 4 primary is effectively tomorrow — gone as a sales window. The real window is the **Nov 3, 2026 general: Kansas signs are statutorily protected Sept 19 – Nov 5.** The ballot is unusually rich: an open Kansas governor's race where Johnson County is the swing battleground (Overland Park = premium inventory), a US Senate race, open MO-6, and KCMO's $1.7B bond package.
- **Honest demand check:** yard signs move ~1 point of vote share (Green/Gerber/Coppock field experiments) and local campaigns are price-sensitive. The winning sales pitch is not "rent a lawn" — it's "**guaranteed placement on the 20 highest-traffic corners in the district**," priced per corner on real AADT. Campaigns can't buy that anywhere today.
- **The sequencing insight:** use the Nov 2026 general as the host-acquisition engine — campaigns fund putting Yardtize placements on premium corners — then on Nov 6, convert that host network to year-round home-services advertisers (the durable, non-seasonal revenue). Politics is the cash spike and the growth hack; services are the base business.

## 4. The Two-City Rulebook (build this into the product)

| | Kansas City, MO | Overland Park, KS |
|---|---|---|
| Max sign size | 8 sq ft, 4 ft tall | 9 sq ft aggregate across ≤3 stake signs |
| Count | 1 commercial-interim; noncommercial to 16 sq ft aggregate | 3 stake signs (+1 corner lots) |
| Duration | none stated | ~60 days (verify §18.440.130) |
| Setback | 5 ft from ROW; corner diagonal 20 ft | ~1 ft behind sidewalk (per realtor table) |
| Permit | none under 10 sq ft | none expected (verify §18.440.030) |
| Political window | +6 wks/−2 wks: unlimited noncommercial count | Sept 19–Nov 5, 2026: city cannot limit count/placement (K.S.A. 25-2711) |
| Paid 3rd-party commercial | prohibited (off-premise) | unresolved — post-2020 code may be purely physical; **verify §18.440.020/.180/.200** |
| Enforcement | 311 complaint → letter → 10–30 days to cure → fine ($50–$1,000 range/day, unverified) | notice → deadline → $140 reinspection fees → up to $500/day at court discretion |
| Enforcer | Neighborhood Preservation (816-513-1313) | Code Compliance, Community Services |

This table *is* the seed of the jurisdiction rules engine: address → city → allowed size/count/duration/setback → what inventory Yardtize offers there. Two cities now; every new city is a data entry, not a code rewrite.

**The Overland Park wildcard — RESOLVED (verified from the live code, Aug 3, 2026; see Addendum):** OP's §18.440.020.A *does* prohibit "Off-site Commercial Signs" — defined as commercial content referring to a *for-profit* business/product/service not on the premises — so a paid ad for an off-site business is prohibited in OP just as in KCMO, payment irrelevant. BUT the verified text opened three doors we didn't know existed: (1) §18.440.130.G expressly allows one additional temporary sign on residential **corner lots** every week from **Friday 6 a.m. to Sunday 9 p.m.** (3 sq ft/face, 2 faces, 4 ft) — and §18.440.020.A explicitly carves this out of the off-site commercial prohibition, making it a *legal weekend commercial ad product on corner lots*; (2) the off-site prohibition only covers **for-profit** advertisers — nonprofits, churches, schools, youth sports, and community events are outside the definition entirely; (3) political/noncommercial signs ride the stake-sign rules (3 signs, 9 sq ft total — a single sign may be the full 9 sq ft — 6 ft tall, 60 days, then a 30-day gap between display periods).

## 5. What This Changes in the Plan

1. **Political is the wedge, not an afterthought** — legally cleanest category, statutorily protected in Kansas, real willingness-to-pay for premium corners, and a built-in Nov 2026 deadline that creates urgency for the fundraise ("we go live for the general").
2. **The product finds corners, not lawns.** Lead feature: traffic-ranked inventory. The Karnes corner (36–44k vehicles/day at a signal) is the demo listing.
3. **Compliance engine is the moat and the pitch** — size/setback/duration validation per jurisdiction, disclaimer checks, W-9/1099 handling, 48-hour takedown SLA, fine guarantee.
4. **Demo storyline for investors:** drop a pin at 3103 Karnes → satellite view → "33,316 + 11,070 vehicles/day, signalized" → suggested rate → "and here's the compliance card showing exactly why this placement is legal." No competitor can show anything like that screen.

## Addendum — Primary-Text Verification (browser session, Aug 3, 2026)

All previously flagged open items were verified directly from the live official code sites.

### Overland Park (codes.opkansas.org, Ch. 18.440, current text)

- **§18.440.130.B (Temporary Signs — Residential), verbatim substance:** stake signs, up to **3 per lot; 9 sq ft for a single sign or 3 sq ft each for multiple, max 9 sq ft total; 6 ft height; 60-day time limit**, with **30 days between display periods**. Non-illuminated temporary signs need no permit. Signs are "the responsibility of the property owner… and presumed also the responsibility of" the entity the sign relates to.
- **§18.440.130.G (the weekend corner-lot sign):** corner lots in residential districts get **one additional temporary sign**: ≤3 sq ft/face (max 2 faces), ≤4 ft tall, displayable **each week Friday 6 a.m. – Sunday 9 p.m.**
- **§18.440.020.A (Prohibited):** "Off-site Commercial Signs" prohibited — **except** billboards by special use permit (§.120.B), sports-venue signs (§.070.B), and **"off-site temporary signs allowed by 18.440.130.G"** — i.e., the weekend corner sign is an express legal channel for off-site commercial content.
- **§18.440.200 (Definitions):** *Off-site commercial sign* = "a sign with commercial content that refers to a **for-profit** business, organization, product, activity or service that is not located or available on the premises." *Stake sign* = "a temporary, rigid sign constructed of plastic, vinyl, metal, or wood." *Temporary sign* examples expressly include "political signs… contractor signs, real estate signs." Nothing anywhere keys on compensation.
- **§18.440.180 (Substitution):** the chapter "regulates only the sign location, structure and copy design and not sign content"; any authorized sign may carry noncommercial copy in lieu of other copy (one-way substitution), and the chapter yields to any express state/federal authorization (i.e., K.S.A. 25-2711's election window).
- **§18.440.190 (Penalties):** violations are a public offense; penalty may be applied to "the person or entity placing the sign, **the business or entity advertised in the sign**, or the property owner" — advertiser-side liability exists; each day is a separate offense.

**Verified strategic upshot for OP:** political/noncommercial = 3 signs/9 sq ft/60 days year-round + unlimited count during the statutory election window; for-profit commercial = corner-lot weekends only (a real product: "your sign on 40 corner lots every weekend"), plus an entirely unregulated-by-this-prohibition lane for **nonprofit** advertisers. The 60-day + 30-day-gap rhythm should be built into the platform's scheduling engine.

### Kansas City, MO (Municode, current text)

- **§88-810-1770 (verbatim):** "SIGN, OUTDOOR ADVERTISING: A sign which directs attention to a business; commodity, service, activity, or product sold, conducted, or offered **off the premises where such sign is located**." Pure premises test; payment nowhere in the definition — confirming the earlier analysis.
- **§88-445-03 (verbatim substance):** any allowed sign may carry "any lawful noncommercial message" in lieu of other copy; the city's stated policy is content-neutral regulation of noncommercial signs. One-way substitution confirmed.

### Grass Spaces (grass-spaces.com, browsed Aug 3, 2026)

Their FAQ (updated the same day) reveals the whole model: advertisers pay an **$8.99 platform access fee** plus a payment "directly to the homeowner"; homeowners "typically earn **$29.99–$39.99 per week**." **Political signs are not permitted** ("Not yet"). On compliance: "**Grass Spaces is not responsible** for sign regulations or permits… Users are fully responsible… contact your local Planning and Zoning Department." Site shows placeholder listings and "what users are looking forward to" testimonials (anticipatory, not actual) — pre-traction. Three takeaways: (1) their $30–40/**week** asking rate validates pricing well above the informal market's $10–50/month; (2) they punt on exactly the two things that make Yardtize defensible — compliance and traffic data; (3) they've excluded the political category that is legally cleanest and seasonally hottest.
